Applying

Who is eligible for a service dog?

Canadian Armed Forces and RCMP veterans and serving members who have been diagnosed with an Operational Stress Injury (OSI) or PTSD as a result of their military or policing service, and who live in Atlantic Canada (Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, or Newfoundland and Labrador).

How do I apply?

Complete the intake form on our Apply page. The form helps us confirm your eligibility. If you're accepted, we'll contact you within a week to continue the process.

Training and costs

Where do the dogs come from?

Every dog is trained and certified by CARES, Inc. (Canine Assistance, Rehabilitation and Education Services) in Concordia, Kansas. CARES has placed nearly 2,000 assistance dogs since 1993, and Paws Fur Thought represents CARES for pairing service dogs with veterans in Atlantic Canada.

What does the training involve?

You'll attend a mandatory 5-day training session with your service dog at CARES in Concordia, Kansas. You learn to work with your dog as a team before you bring them home.

What does it cost me?

Paws Fur Thought funds the cost of the service dog, your flights, and your accommodation in Concordia. You'll need to rent a car to drive from the Wichita airport to Concordia, about 2 hours and 15 minutes. [CONFIRM: is the rental car reimbursed? Are meals covered?]

About service dogs

Is a service dog the same as an emotional support animal?

No. A service dog is trained and tested for public access and task-trained to help their handler with a specific disability, such as OSI/PTSD intervention. In Nova Scotia, service dogs are covered by the Service Dog Act.

Can I pet a service dog I see in public?

Please don't pet, call, or feed a working service dog without asking the handler first. The dog is on the job, and distractions can make it harder for them to help their handler.
